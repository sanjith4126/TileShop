Tile Calculator: How Many Tiles Do I Need?

Multiply the room's length by its width to get the area, add about 10% extra for cutting and breakage, then divide by the area of one tile. For example, a 10 × 12 ft room is 120 sq.ft; with 10% extra that is 132 sq.ft, which is 33 tiles of 2 × 2 ft, or 35 tiles of 60 × 60 cm.

Tiles are often sold in boxes. Check how many tiles or square feet a box holds and round up to whole boxes.

How the calculation works

  1. Room area = length × width. Measure in feet; for an L-shaped room, split it into rectangles and add them up.
  2. Add extra: multiply by 1.10 for 10% (1.15 for 15%). The extra covers tiles that are cut at the edges and any that break.
  3. Area of one tile: a 60 × 60 cm tile is about 3.88 sq.ft (1 sq.ft = 929 sq.cm).
  4. Tiles needed = area with extra ÷ area of one tile, rounded up.
